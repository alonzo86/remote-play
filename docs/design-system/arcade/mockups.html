Wobble,
arcade edition

Five ways to take the TV app and the phone controller toward an old arcade, pixel-art feel. Each one shows at least the TV Connect screen and the phone during a game. A and C are also compared screen by screen, and D and E add the Games menu and the results. These are mockups to react to, not final designs. The player colors are your current ones so the comparison stays fair.

  • Pac-Man, Donkey Kong cabinets

Good

Watch out

What it would mean in Godot

Pixel fonts on a TV

  • Pixel fonts look sharp only at whole-number multiples of their grid. At 1080p that means sizes like 32, 40, 48 px, with antialiasing off and nearest filtering.
  • The current rule (all TV text at least 28 px) still holds. Pixel fonts need a little more, so body text would move to about 32 px.
  • All the fonts shown are free (OFL) and can sit in assets/fonts like Lilita One and Fredoka do now.

Pixel UI with 3D games

  • Option 1: games render at a low resolution (for example 480x270) and scale up with nearest filtering. Marble Race and the others become pixel-art for free, but fine detail is lost.
  • Option 2: games keep smooth rendering and only the shell (HUD, countdown, results, pause) goes pixel. This is cheaper, and the tier rules (FD-14) already allow it.
  • A per-game choice is possible too: tier 3 is free.

What changes in code

  • UiTheme colors and fonts, and the ui_kit styleboxes (corner radius to 0, a stepped border or a 9-patch pixel frame).
  • FD-13 (Party Pop) would be replaced by a new decision, and tokens.json regenerated.
  • The game tiles would need new art to match. Pixel tiles could be drawn in code or as small PNGs scaled up.

My suggestion

Start with C, Pixel Pop for the menus and the phone, and keep the games smooth for now. It gets you the arcade feel with the least risk to readability, and you can push further toward A later, one screen at a time, if C feels too soft. If you want the real thing from day one, A is the honest arcade look, but plan on making the games pixel-art too.
